Mastering Workflow

Process

Mastering is where a record becomes itself — the final stage where technical precision and critical listening converge. Every decision is made in service of the music: how it feels, how it translates, and how it holds up across every format and playback environment it will ever encounter.

The Work

  • Evaluate the full project for tonal balance, dynamics, and emotional intent before any processing begins
  • Apply equalization, compression, and limiting with purpose — only what the music actually needs
  • Optimize loudness for streaming platforms while preserving dynamic range and feel
  • Create consistency across the full album or EP without flattening what makes each track distinct
  • Sequence tracks, shape transitions, and control spacing to support the project as a whole
  • Prepare stems, instrumentals, and alternate versions as needed
  • Edit and remove unwanted noise, clicks, or technical artifacts
  • Apply restoration and noise reduction for archival, reissue, or tape transfer work
  • Deliver streaming-ready masters meeting current platform loudness targets
  • Prepare vinyl pre-masters with appropriate frequency and dynamic adjustments for the format
  • Embed accurate ISRC codes and metadata across all deliverables
  • Perform quality control checks on every file before delivery
  • Archive all project files with organized, labeled backups

Quality Control

Every master is evaluated for tonal balance, stereo imaging, dynamics, and translation across playback environments — from professional monitors to earbuds to car speakers. Technical compliance and metadata integrity are verified across all delivery formats before anything leaves.

Delivery Formats

WAV / AIFF Streaming Masters Vinyl Pre-Masters Spatial Audio Stems & Alternates Archival Transfers

Monitoring

Masters are referenced across multiple playback environments throughout the process — not just at the end. Translation is built in, not checked after the fact. The goal is a record that sounds right wherever it's heard.
