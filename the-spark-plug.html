Three Bands

Saturation belongs to a frequency range.

Drive a full-range signal and the low end decides how much harmonic content everything else gets. The Spark Plug splits the signal at two crossover points you choose — Low/Mid anywhere from 60 Hz to 900 Hz, Mid/High from 1.1 kHz to 12 kHz — and gives each region its own character mode, its own drive, and its own blend against its own dry signal. Weight in the lows, presence in the mids, air or bite on top, each decided on its own terms.

Character
One of four harmonic curves per band — Soft, Warm, Punchy or Vintage. Three bands can run three different curves at once.
Drive
0 to 24 dB into that band's curve. How hard the band is pushed, and nothing else in the plug-in changes with it.
Mix
0% to 100% per band, blending that band's saturated signal against its own dry contribution. Parallel saturation, three times over.
Solo
Hear one band, or any combination, while you set it. The dry blend steps aside while a solo is active, so you hear the band and not the mix under it.

The crossovers are Linkwitz-Riley, 24 dB per octave, so the bands overlap through the transition rather than switching at a hard frequency split. They default to 250 Hz and 3 kHz, and the frequency printed on each divider is the control — drag it, or right-click to type an exact value.

Character

Four curves, not four presets.

Each mode is a different harmonic transfer curve, and each is normalized so that at 0 dB Drive it is not quietly making the band louder. What you hear when you change modes is the harmonic content changing, not the level.

Soft
A gentle symmetric round-off. Linear through the middle of its range, so at low drive it leaves the band alone and only begins to shape the signal as you push it.
Warm
An asymmetric curve with even-harmonic content that grows with level and fades as the signal falls away, rather than sitting at a fixed amount regardless of what you feed it.
Punchy
Transient-reactive. It crossfades between a gentle and a more aggressive curve according to what the signal is doing, with added presence, so attacks keep their edge.
Vintage
Even-harmonic tape-style saturation with high-frequency softening and a nonlinear low-end resonance, for thickness that darkens slightly as it builds.

All nonlinear processing runs oversampled, at a factor fixed by your session's sample rate, so the harmonics the curves generate stay where they belong instead of folding back down the spectrum.

The Output

Level stays your decision.

Drive is a gain into a curve, so a band that sounds right is usually louder than the one you started with. The Spark Plug gives you two separate answers to that, and keeps them separate.

Auto Gain
Off by default. When on, it measures the processed signal against the unprocessed band sum and compensates the difference, so you can judge drive and character by tone rather than by loudness.
Output Trim
±24 dB, applied after Auto Gain and never touched by it. The number in the field is always the one you set — nothing rewrites it behind you.
Global Mix
0% to 100% over the whole plug-in, blending the unprocessed signal against everything the three bands did. Independent of the per-band Mix controls, which stay exactly as you set them.
Metering
Stereo peak meters on the input and the output, with peak hold. A channel that went over keeps a red marker on its own well until you click to clear it.

A fixed output stage holds the plug-in's own output at full scale so a hard drive setting cannot leave the plug-in above 0 dBFS. It has no control, never touches a signal below −1 dBFS, and is protection rather than a mastering limiter — the clip indicator lights exactly when it is doing anything at all.

What is The Spark Plug?

A three-band harmonic saturator. The signal is split into Low, Mid and High at two crossover points you set, and each band gets its own character mode, drive amount and dry/wet blend, so the harmonics added to one region are independent of the others.

Why three bands instead of one saturator?

Because a full-range drive lets the loudest part of the signal, usually the low end, decide how much harmonic content everything else receives. Three bands let you add weight down low without dulling the top, or add bite on top without thickening the bass.

What is the difference between the four character modes?

Each is a different harmonic transfer curve. Soft is a gentle symmetric round-off that stays out of the way at low drive. Warm is asymmetric, with even-harmonic content that grows with level. Punchy reacts to transients, crossfading toward a more aggressive curve with added presence. Vintage is tape-style even-harmonic saturation with high-frequency softening and a nonlinear low-end resonance.

Does Auto Gain change my Output Trim?

No. Auto Gain measures the processed signal against the unprocessed band sum and applies its compensation before Output Trim, so the number in the Output Trim field is always the one you set. It is off by default, and switching it off returns the plug-in to exactly the level it had without it.

Is the output stage a limiter?

No. It is a fixed safety stage that keeps the plug-in's own output from exceeding full scale at extreme drive settings. It has no control, it never touches a signal below −1 dBFS, and the clip indicator lights exactly when it is doing anything. It is not a mastering limiter and is not intended as one.

Does it add latency?

Yes, a small fixed amount from the oversampled nonlinear stage. It is reported to the host, which compensates for it, and bypassed output is the input delayed by exactly that amount so a bypass comparison stays aligned.

What formats are supported?

VST3 and AU on macOS (Apple Silicon & Intel, Universal). AAX / Windows are not currently supported.

How many computers can I install it on?

Up to 2 computers that you own or control.

Will there be updates?

The Spark Plug checks once, shortly after you open it, for a newer stable version. If one is available, an UPDATE AVAILABLE badge appears; clicking it opens this page. The checker only reads the public version manifest—it never downloads or installs software and does not transmit license, project, or usage data. If the check cannot be completed, nothing appears.
